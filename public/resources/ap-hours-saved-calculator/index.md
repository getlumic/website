## The formulas, in plain text.

Every figure in this AP automation savings calculator comes from these four lines. They work the same on paper.

### Hours per month = (B × M + B × E × X) ÷ 60

B is bills per month, M is minutes per straight-through bill, E is the exception rate as a share (20% is 0.20), and X is the extra minutes per exception.

### Cost per month = hours per month × H

H is the loaded hourly cost: pay plus taxes and benefits, for the people who do the work.

### Year = month × 12

The same for hours and for cost.

### Cost per bill = cost per month ÷ B

The cost of one bill, straight-through and exceptions together.

Worked with the example numbers. (200 × 8 + 200 × 0.20 × 15) ÷ 60 = 2,200 ÷ 60 ≈ 36.7 hours a month. 2,200 × $45 ÷ 60 = $1,650 a month; $1,650 × 12 = $19,800 a year; $1,650 ÷ 200 = $8.25 a bill.

## What drives invoice processing time.

Straight-through bills. A straight-through bill matches the purchase order and the receipt. Someone still opens it, reads the vendor, the lines and the total, keys it in and files the copy. A few minutes a bill looks small; multiplied by every bill in a month, it adds up.

Exceptions. A price that differs from the PO, a short shipment, a missing PO number or a duplicate takes a person away from the queue to call the vendor, ask the buyer and wait for an answer. A small share of bills can take a large share of the time, so the exception rate and the extra minutes move the result more than they seem to.

Retyping into a second system. When the PO lives in one system and the payables in another, each bill is keyed twice and the two are checked against each other. That can double the straight-through minutes and adds exceptions of its own.
